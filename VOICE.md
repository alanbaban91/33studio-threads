# 33 Studio: Tone of Voice

We write like the agencies whose work ends up on the Cannes stage: Ogilvy, Saatchi & Saatchi,
M&C Saatchi, McCann, Wolff Olins. Not like an agency that posts "5 tips to grow your brand 🚀".

## The one rule
**One idea, said so simply it can't be misunderstood.** If a post needs two ideas, it's two posts.
(M&C Saatchi calls it brutal simplicity. Ogilvy said it as "the headline is 80 cents of your dollar".)

## Five principles
1. **Truth first.** Every post starts from something true that clients feel but rarely say.
   We say it out loud. That's the hook.
2. **Headline-led.** The first line has to work alone, like a poster. If someone reads only that
   line, they should still get it.
3. **Wit, not jokes.** We're clever about the work, never at someone's expense. A smile, not a laugh track.
4. **Confidence is quiet.** No exclamation marks, no emoji, no "amazing", no hashtags.
   Strong brands don't shout; they don't have to.
5. **Behaviour over decoration.** We talk about ideas, decisions and how brands act.
   We never brag about pretty pictures or our awards. The thinking is the flex.

## Our three moods
| Mood | What it does | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Friendly** | Warm and human. Respects founders, celebrates the craft, invites a conversation. | "To the founder who designed their own sign: respect." |
| **Roast** | Calls out a lazy habit in branding and marketing. Roasts the habit, never a person or a named brand. | "“Clean” is what you call a design you can't remember." |
| **Guide** | Gives away real craft knowledge, generously, before asking for anything. | "Write the problem, not the order." |

These moods still apply, but inside the five culture territories below. Commerce posts are the exception, not the rule.

## Culture first, content second (strategy, October 2026)
**Diagnosis.** Our feed was a service catalogue: every post explains what we sell and asks for
something. Nobody reposts a catalogue. People don't follow brands for information; they follow
them to belong to something. Content fills a feed. Culture gives people a reason to come back,
to reply, and to say "this is us."

**Our cultural stance.** For years, creative work here has been told it must look like Dubai or
Europe to count as professional. We believe the opposite: our streets, our script and our humour
are the advantage. The most interesting brands of the next decade will look like where they're
from. 33 Studio is the studio that notices Sulaymaniyah, and makes it proud of how it looks.
(Website line: "We don't just tell stories. We shift perspectives.")

**Five territories** (every post lives in one):
| Territory | What it is | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| **The City** | Slemani as muse: hand-painted signs, bazaar colour, tea houses, autumn light, Goyzha. Observed with affection, never exoticised. | "Every bazaar has a colour palette nobody designed." |
| **The Script** | Kurdish letters as design material. Love letters to the alphabet, type opinions. | "One of the most beautiful scripts to design with, and one of the least designed for." |
| **The Craft** | Inside the studio, unpolished: sketches, killed ideas, arguments, rituals. | "We killed our favourite idea today. It was clever. It wasn't true." |
| **The Scene** | Give the stage away: questions to the community, students, local creatives (only with their permission). | "Who's the most underrated creative in Kurdistan right now?" |
| **The Take** | Opinions on taste, AI, the region, the industry. Generous, provocative, never cynical. | "AI will make average free. Good news for anyone willing to be specific." |

**Rituals.** Recurring series give people an appointment:
- *Slemani Type*: one sign, letter or shopfront from the city (needs a real photo).
- *Studio Notes*: one honest line from inside the studio.
- *Open Desk*: one question to the community, every week.
- *Kill List*: an idea we killed, and why.

**The ratio.** 80% culture, 20% commerce. Culture posts carry no ask; the profile link does that
work. At most one post in five mentions a service or 33studio.org.

**The pace.** Culture rewards scarcity. Two strong posts a day beat five forgettable ones.
Five a day reads as a content farm, which is the opposite of the brand.

**What we measure.** Replies, reposts, quotes, saves and DMs. Not likes, not reach.

**Guardrails.** No politics, no religion. Affection, not nostalgia. Never exoticise our own
city. Ask before featuring a person or their work. Check every fact about the city or language.

## The ask
- Quiet, specific and last. One of: `33studio.org`, `hello@33studio.org`, "we're on Salim Street".
- Most posts don't sell. At most one in five carries an ask (see the culture ratio above).
- Never: "DM us now", "link in bio", "limited spots", "don't miss out".

## Words we don't use
amazing, stunning, elevate, unlock, game-changer, cutting-edge, synergy, passionate,
"in today's fast-paced world", "let's dive in", "we've got you covered", any emoji, any hashtag.

## Facts we can use
Sulaymaniyah, Kurdistan · Salim Street · made by hand: sketched first, argued over, redrawn,
then built · we ask the awkward questions early · Kurdish, Arabic and English · services:
brand strategy & identity, graphic design, illustration & ad concepts, motion, TVC & video,
AI TVC, AI workflows, 3D & VFX, social & content systems, web & app.
Don't invent clients, numbers, awards or years in business.
**Never name or quote clients.** No client names, logos, testimonials or project details. We talk about what we do, not who we did it for.

## Check before it goes in the queue
- Would the first line work as a poster?
- Could a competitor post this unchanged? If yes, rewrite it.
- Is there a word you could cut? Cut it.
- Did we say one thing, or three?
